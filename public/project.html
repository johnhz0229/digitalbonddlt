Settling a tokenised bond across two ledgers

Delivery versus payment and payment versus discharge without time-locks or a central orchestrator

Zheng Huang · Source code

Abstract

This project implements the life cycle of a fixed-rate bond whose units are held on one ledger (the asset ledger) while its cash moves on another (the payment ledger). Every cash flow, whether issuance price, coupon or principal, is settled with the same protocol: the payer locks cash against two hashes, and a stateless oracle reveals exactly one preimage depending on the outcome. The bond records an obligation as fulfilled only when it accepts the success preimage. The implementation consists of four Solidity contracts, five event-driven off-chain participants and an automated test suite.

1 Problem

When the two legs of a trade settle at different times, the party that delivers first bears the full value at risk (principal risk). On a single ledger, both legs can be executed in one atomic transaction. If the asset and the cash are recorded on different ledgers, no transaction spans both.

Hash time-locked contracts bridge two ledgers with deadlines. This introduces timing risk and gives the party holding the secret an option to wait. A second problem concerns servicing: a payment status reported by another system should not, on its own, discharge an obligation recorded on the asset ledger.

Question. How can issuance, coupons and redemption be settled across two ledgers so that no party bears principal risk, no time-lock is needed, failures are recoverable, and obligations are discharged only by verifiable proof?

2 Design

2.1 Participants

ParticipantActsResponsibility
Issuermanuallypublishes indicative coupons per tenor, sets final terms, opens coupon settlements
Investormanuallyrequests a trade, confirms the terms, locks the purchase price, requests redemption
ISIN agenton eventsassigns an identifier once the terms are confirmed
Registraron eventsdeploys the bond token, keeps the eligibility register, mints units
Decryption oracleon eventsgenerates key pairs, later decrypts exactly the requested key; keeps no trade state
Issuer / investor connectoron eventsverify incoming cash locks, lock outgoing cash, claim or recover cash

Each participant signs with its own key and reacts only to on-chain events. Contracts on the two ledgers never call each other.

2.2 Life cycle

A factory holds the issuer's indicative coupons per tenor. A trade request deploys one contract instance per trade, which then moves through an explicit state machine; each transition is allowed for one role only.

IndicationPhase ─inceptTrade (issuer)──────▶ TradeIncepted
TradeIncepted   ─confirmTrade (investor)───▶ DistributionPhase   terms immutable
DistributionPhase ─assignIsin (ISIN agent)─▶ IsinAssigned
IsinAssigned    ─registerToken (registrar)─▶ Minted
Minted          ─issuance settled──────────▶ Issued
Issued          ─coupons, redemption───────▶ Redeemed

2.3 Settlement protocol

Issuance, every coupon and the principal use the same seven steps. ks and kf denote the success and failure key; H is keccak256.

  asset ledger                         oracle            payment ledger
  ───────────────────────────────────  ────────────────  ──────────────────────────
1 request keys ───────────────────────▶ generate k_s, k_f
                                        publish H(k), Enc(k); discard k
2                                                         payer locks cash
                                                          against H(k_s), H(k_f)
3 payee's connector verifies the lock ◀────────────────── and attests it
4 execute asset leg, request ONE key ─▶
5                                       check key document
                                        (contract, settlement, payee,
                                         amount, outcome); publish key
6 accept preimage:
   k_s → obligation discharged
   k_f → attempt closed, obligation open
7                                                         k_s → cash to payee
                                                          k_f → cash back to payer

2.4 Intended properties

  1. P1 No principal risk. The asset leg executes only after an irrevocable cash lock has been verified by the party that would lose from a fake one.
  2. P2 One outcome. At most one key is requested and published per settlement attempt.
  3. P3 Discharge by proof. An obligation is fulfilled only when the bond accepts the success preimage.
  4. P4 Recoverable failure. A failure key returns the locked cash and closes the attempt, never the obligation; a new attempt uses fresh keys.
  5. P5 No time-locks. Nothing depends on deadlines; an unpaid attempt can be aborted at any time.
  6. P6 Bound keys. Nobody knows a preimage before release, and each key names the payee and amount it may be released for.

Solidity 0.8.24; two in-memory EVM ledgers (ganache) with chain ids 7101 and 7102; ethers v6; Hardhat for tests. Keys are JSON documents encrypted with RSA-OAEP (3072 bit); the asset ledger stores their keccak256 hashes. The terms are a structured record whose hash the investor confirms after checking it against the values the contract enforces.

    5 Evaluation

    Each property is covered by automated tests (npm test).

    PropertyTest
    P1the issuer's connector refuses a wrong cash lock; only the payee can attest a cash lock
    P2only the oracle can publish a key, and only the requested one
    P3a coupon is only discharged when the bond accepts the success key
    P4the investor aborts, recovers the cash and retries
    P5full life cycle without any deadline: issuance, two coupons, redemption with burn
    P6the oracle refuses a request whose terms differ from the key; a buyer paying one cent to his own second address gets no key
    Life cycleeach step needs the right role and the right state; coupons and redemption are not due early; terms are confirmed only if record and contract agree

    An earlier version keyed payments by transfer id only, so a buyer could pay one cent to his own second address and still obtain the success key. The fix binds every key to its payee and amount (P6); the attack is now a regression test. The full suite, including earlier single-ledger and two-ledger modules, has 54 passing tests.

    6 Limitations

    • Local ledgers only; the payment ledger models a hash-locked account and is not a payment system.
    • No legal register or real identifier; eligibility is a flag set by the registrar.
    • One oracle key: the oracle is a single point of trust and a liveness dependency. Threshold decryption would address the first.
    • Attestation of the cash lock by the payee is a design assumption.
    • One investor per instance, no secondary trading, regular annual coupon periods only, no business-day convention.
    • Not audited.
